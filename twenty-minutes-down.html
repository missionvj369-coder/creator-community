Twenty Minutes Down: Sleep

Nothing to write. Nothing to choose. Twenty minutes of guided settling, then a soft tone that fades to silence by minute forty.

Lie down, keep the volume just audible, and close your eyes whenever you like. A soft tone rises as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. This is a relaxation exercise, not medical treatment. Do not use it while driving. If you feel dizzy, breathe normally. If you have a sleep disorder or health condition, talk to a clinician.

How it works, and what the evidence says

Calm music. A 2022 meta-analysis of 13 randomised trials (1,007 adults with insomnia) found that listening to music improved sleep quality. A 2025 review found the most effective music was slow (about 60 to 80 beats a minute, or no beat), soft, smooth, instrumental and simple.

Slow breathing. A 2001 BMJ study of 23 healthy adults found that prayer and mantra recitation slowed breathing to about six a minute and increased heart-rate variability. The session paces breathing near that rate.

Why the sound ends in silence. A 2026 laboratory study of 25 adults found that pink noise played during sleep reduced REM sleep by about 19 minutes. So this session uses a little noise only while you are awake, fades it out, and leaves nothing playing through the night.

Not proven here. The exact timings, the 20 and 40 minute lengths, the tones, the dim warm colours and the spoken body scan are design choices. I did not use "healing frequencies" or binaural beats because I have not seen reliable evidence for them.
