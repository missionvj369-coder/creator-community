THE STILL ROOM
Stay at rest · 20 minutes

00:00 — Welcome to the Still Room
Welcome to the Still Room. Find a position you can stay in comfortably. For these few minutes, nothing needs to be finished.

00:24 — Let the surface hold you
Notice the support beneath you. The chair, the bed, or the ground can take some of your weight. You can keep your eyes open.

00:45 — An ordinary breath
Notice one ordinary breath. Let it arrive without pulling it deeper. Let it leave without pushing. There is no breath to hold.

01:05 — A rhythm that fits you
If it feels easy, let the breath become a little slower. Keep it small and comfortable. Your own rhythm is always enough.

01:28 — Nothing to force
You do not need to follow the light or any count. If you feel uncomfortable, return to your usual breathing, or pause whenever you wish.

01:57 — You can let the view go
Let your breathing keep its own rhythm now. If it feels comfortable, soften your gaze, or close your eyes. There is nothing more you need to watch.

02:23 — Begin with the feet
Bring a little attention to your feet. Perhaps there is warmth, pressure, or very little to notice. There is no special feeling to find.

02:42 — Let the legs rest
Notice your lower legs and your thighs. You can leave them as they are. If it is comfortable, let the surface beneath take their weight.

03:01 — Support beneath your body
Notice the hips and the back. There may be points of contact with the surface beneath you. Nothing needs to be pressed down or held still.

03:20 — Hands with nothing to carry
Let attention reach the hands and arms. They can rest wherever they are comfortable. For this moment, there is nothing they need to carry.

03:38 — A little less effort
Notice the shoulders, the jaw, and the space around the eyes. Perhaps a little effort can soften. If it cannot, that is all right too.

03:58 — The body as a whole
You may notice the body as a whole, breathing in its own rhythm. Rest with a sound, a point of contact, or simply this moment.

04:18 — Thoughts can come and go
If thoughts arrive, you have not done anything wrong. Let them come and go. You can return gently to the feeling of support beneath you.

04:40 — You can stay at rest
You can stay at rest. There is no need to open your eyes or check the screen. Sleep may come, or this can simply be a quiet pause.

05:20 — A little more time
There is a little more time here. Nothing new needs to happen. You may notice the support beneath you, with no need to change anything.

05:50 — A rhythm of your own
Your breathing can keep its own rhythm. There is no count to follow now, and no particular size of breath that you need to make.

06:22 — Room for wandering
If attention has wandered, that is part of being here. A thought can be present while the feeling of support is present too.

06:56 — Whatever is here
There may be warmth, coolness, pressure, or very little to notice. Any of these is enough. Your experience does not need to match the words.

07:32 — Hands at rest
Perhaps your hands are resting somewhere comfortable. They do not need a special position. There may be a little less work for them to do.

08:10 — Sounds around you
Sounds may come and go around you. They need not become another task. If one catches your attention, returning to the body remains an option.

08:50 — An ordinary breath
A comfortable exhalation may finish by itself. The next breath can arrive in its own time. Nothing needs holding, stretching, or making deliberately slower.

09:32 — The body as a whole
You may be aware of the body as a whole, rather than finding each part. There is no need to search for a special sensation.

10:16 — A thought can wait
If a thought asks for an answer, you do not have to solve it in this moment. Noticing that it arrived can be enough.

11:02 — Support remains
The surface beneath you continues to be there. Some weight may already be resting on it. There is no need to test whether you feel relaxed.

11:50 — An imperfect pause
Your face may feel as it does. Your shoulders may feel as they do. Comfort does not have to be complete for a pause to be allowed.

12:40 — No test to pass
There may be moments of quiet and moments with many thoughts. Neither one decides how well you are doing. There is nothing here to pass.

13:32 — Your choices remain
If any part of this is uncomfortable, changing position, opening your eyes, or stopping remains available. Rest can include making a small adjustment for yourself.

14:26 — Fewer words
The words will become less frequent now. You may stay with an ordinary sound, the support beneath you, or simply the feeling of being here.

15:22 — A quiet pause
Sleep may come, or this may remain a quiet pause. There is no need to wait for a sign, and no need to make either happen.

16:30 — Nothing more to follow
There is nothing more to follow. The sound will soften on its own. If you wish, this can simply be a little time without another task.
